Autumn Reading Plan

Serious Reading Classics

Every Sunday I attend the Munich Silent Book Club. It is called silent for a reason — no one discusses what they are reading, no one recommends, no one asks how far along you are. And yet, somehow, the quiet is the most motivating thing about it. To sit in a room full of strangers who have all chosen to be alone with a book, together — that is its own kind of encouragement.

2026 has not been a generous reading year. I have been mood-reading, and moods, as it turns out, change as quickly as everything else. I noticed another pattern too: I kept reaching for scriptures, or for books I needed to reread the moment I finished them.

I am however incredibly proud of what I have read this year so far. I finished the Bhagavad Gita - for the first time in my life. I am equally proud of getting past the first chapter of The Sound and the Fury, which is no small thing.

Still, I owe something to the books I started and set down. Before the year draws in and I go into my usual hibernation, I want to return to them — deliberately, slowly, without waiting for the right mood to arrive.

Here is the list. Five books I intend to finish. I will fill in the dates as I go.
